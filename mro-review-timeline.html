What Is an MRO?

An MRO (Medical Review Officer) is a licensed physician trained and certified under 49 CFR Part 40 to review all DOT drug test laboratory results. The MRO is not part of the collection process — they receive results directly from the SAMHSA-certified lab and are the final decision-maker on every result.

The MRO exists to protect donors from false positives. If a test comes back positive from the lab, the MRO contacts you privately to determine whether a legitimate medical explanation exists — a valid prescription, for example — before reporting the result to the employer. The employer sees only the final MRO determination, not the raw lab result.

Employer cannot see a positive result until after MRO review

Federal regulations prohibit the lab from reporting directly to the employer. The MRO is the only authorized party to report a verified result — positive or negative — to the DER (Designated Employer Representative).

Step-by-Step DOT Drug Test Timeline

From collection to reported result — what happens at each stage and how long each takes.

1
Specimen Collection
Day 0

Donor provides observed or unobserved urine specimen at a DOT-certified collection site. Collector completes the federal custody and control form (CCF). Specimen is sealed, labeled, and shipped to a SAMHSA-certified laboratory — usually same-day or overnight.

2
Lab Initial Screening (Immunoassay)
Day 1–2

The lab runs an initial immunoassay screen on all 5 substances. Negative screens go directly to the MRO for review. Non-negative screens proceed to GC/MS confirmatory testing before going to the MRO — this adds 1 additional business day.

3
MRO Review
Day 2–4
If Negative
MRO verifies and reports negative to the employer's DER. Total time from collection: typically 2–4 business days.
If Non-Negative
MRO contacts donor for interview. Donor has chance to explain (prescription, medical condition). Adds 1–5 days depending on response time.
4
Donor Interview (Non-Negatives Only)
Day 3–8

The MRO calls you directly — not your employer — to discuss the result privately. You may provide documentation of a valid prescription or medical condition. The MRO evaluates whether the explanation is legitimate under DOT rules.

Stay reachable
The MRO must attempt contact at least twice over 24 hours. If they cannot reach you, they contact your employer's DER to assist. Ultimately, the MRO can verify a positive without the interview if all attempts fail.
5
MRO Reports Final Result to Employer

The MRO issues the final determination — negative, positive, cancelled, or test not performed — to the employer's DER. If positive, the result is also reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse. A positive triggers immediate removal from safety-sensitive duties and the start of the Return to Duty process.

Why Is My Result Taking Longer Than Expected?

🔬
GC/MS confirmatory testing
A non-negative initial screen triggers a second, more specific test (gas chromatography/mass spectrometry). This adds 1 additional business day at the lab before going to the MRO. It does not mean you failed — it means the initial screen needs confirmation.
✅
Specimen validity testing
All DOT specimens are tested for validity (creatinine, specific gravity, pH). If the lab flags a specimen as potentially adulterated or substituted, additional testing is required before the MRO can review.
📞
MRO cannot reach the donor
For a non-negative result, the MRO must make multiple attempts to contact you directly. If you have an unknown or disconnected number on file, this delays the donor interview and adds days to the process.
📄
Medical records requested
If you claim a prescription during the donor interview, the MRO may request records from your prescribing doctor to verify. This depends on what you authorize and how quickly the records are sent.
🏠
Insufficient specimen volume
If the specimen volume was too low (shy bladder protocol), a second collection may have been required. If a second collection was not done at the time, the test may be reported as cancelled and require a new collection entirely.

MRO Review FAQ

Can I call the MRO to check on my result? +

Yes. You have the right to contact the MRO directly. Request their contact information from the collection site or your employer's DER. The MRO may not discuss a positive result before the donor interview is complete, but can confirm whether your result is still under review.

What if the MRO calls while I am driving? +

Call back as soon as you can. The MRO will note the time of each contact attempt. You do not need to answer immediately, but delaying the callback extends the review period and — if you remain unreachable — the MRO will eventually involve your employer's DER to locate you.

Does a non-negative screen mean I failed? +

No. A non-negative initial screen goes to GC/MS confirmation. GC/MS is far more specific and can rule out cross-reactivity that caused the initial screen to flag. Some initial screens come back non-negative but confirm as negative after GC/MS — this is reported to the employer as a negative.

What is the split specimen and when should I request it? +

Every DOT specimen is split into two bottles (A and B) at collection. If the MRO reports a confirmed positive, adulterated, or substituted result, you have 72 hours to request that the B bottle be sent to a second SAMHSA-certified lab for re-testing. This is your right under federal regulations. Request it immediately through the MRO — the 72-hour clock starts when the MRO notifies you of the result.

Will my employer know the result before I do? +

For a negative result, the employer is typically notified at the same time or just after you. For a non-negative, the MRO must attempt to contact you privately first — the employer's DER is only contacted if the MRO cannot reach you after reasonable attempts. The MRO does not report a positive to the employer until the donor interview is resolved.
