DOT Drug Test — Know Your Rights

Failed a DOT Drug Test
with a Prescription?

A valid prescription doesn't automatically clear you — but it can. Here's exactly what the Medical Review Officer process looks like, which drugs are and aren't explainable, and what happens to your CDL.

The Most Important Thing to Know First

DOT follows federal law — not state law. A state medical marijuana card provides zero protection against a positive DOT drug test. THC is always a violation under DOT rules, regardless of any prescription or card.

For all other prescription medications, the answer is more nuanced. A valid, verified prescription may result in a canceled (negative) result — but only if the Medical Review Officer (MRO) determines the medication provides a legitimate medical explanation and does not pose a safety risk for safety-sensitive duties.

Never Explainable by Prescription

  • Marijuana / THC (federal Schedule I — no prescription exists federally)
  • Cocaine (no legitimate medical use for CDL drivers)
  • PCP (no legitimate medical use for CDL drivers)
  • Heroin / 6-AM (no legitimate medical use)

Potentially Explainable by Prescription

  • Opioids (hydrocodone, oxycodone, morphine, codeine) — with valid Rx and MRO safety determination
  • Amphetamines — with valid Rx for ADHD (Adderall, Vyvanse) and MRO review
  • Methamphetamine — valid Rx for Desoxyn possible but rare

The MRO Review Process — Step by Step

When a lab returns a non-negative result, it goes to the Medical Review Officer (MRO) — not your employer. The MRO is required by 49 CFR Part 40 to contact you before reporting any violation.

1

Lab Reports Non-Negative to MRO

The SAMHSA-certified lab sends the confirmed non-negative result to the MRO. Your employer does not see this yet.

2

MRO Attempts to Contact You

The MRO must make reasonable attempts to reach you directly within a defined timeframe. Do not ignore MRO calls. If you cannot be reached within 72 hours, the MRO reports a verified positive without your input.

3

Medical Interview

You have the opportunity to provide a legitimate medical explanation — your prescription information, prescribing physician's name, and the medical condition being treated. You may also request a split specimen test at this stage.

4

MRO Verifies Prescription

The MRO contacts your prescribing physician to verify the prescription is current, valid, and prescribed in the normal course of professional treatment. They also evaluate whether the medication affects your ability to safely perform safety-sensitive duties.

5

MRO Makes Determination

If the prescription is valid and no safety concern exists, the MRO reports the result as negative — no violation, no Clearinghouse entry. If the prescription is invalid, unverifiable, or the drug poses a safety risk, the MRO reports a verified positive violation.

6

Employer & Clearinghouse Notified (If Positive)

A verified positive is reported to your employer's DER and to the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse within 3 business days. You must immediately stop safety-sensitive functions.

If you have a prescription for an opioid or amphetamine, contact your prescribing physician before your DOT drug test and ask whether the medication affects your ability to safely drive a CMV. Your physician can prepare documentation in advance.

If Your Result Is Verified Positive — What's Next

1

Stop Safety-Sensitive Functions Immediately

You cannot drive a CMV, operate equipment, or perform any DOT-covered safety-sensitive function until you complete the full return-to-duty process.

2

SAP Evaluation

You must be evaluated by a DOT-qualified Substance Abuse Professional (SAP). The SAP assesses your situation and recommends an education or treatment program.

3

Complete SAP's Recommended Program

Follow through on the education or treatment plan. The SAP determines when you are ready for a follow-up evaluation.

4

SAP Follow-Up Evaluation

The SAP conducts a follow-up evaluation confirming you have complied with the program and are ready to return to safety-sensitive duties.

5

Negative RTD Drug Test

You must pass a directly observed DOT return-to-duty drug test before resuming safety-sensitive functions.

6

Follow-Up Testing Plan

Minimum 6 unannounced follow-up tests over the first 12 months, continuing for up to 5 years per SAP direction. These are in addition to random testing pool requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a valid prescription cancel a DOT drug test failure?

Sometimes. A valid prescription can result in the MRO canceling a non-negative result if it provides a legitimate medical explanation. However, marijuana is never explainable, and opioids/amphetamines require the MRO to also confirm the medication doesn't pose a safety risk for driving.

Does a medical marijuana card protect me from a positive DOT drug test?

No. DOT follows federal law. Marijuana is a federal Schedule I substance. No state medical card or prescription provides any protection — positive THC results are always verified as violations.

What happens if I don't respond to the MRO?

If the MRO cannot reach you within 72 hours, they are required to report a verified positive without your input. Always respond to MRO contact attempts immediately.

Can I request a retest of my specimen?

Yes. You can request a split specimen test within 72 hours of MRO notification. The split (Bottle B) is sent to a second SAMHSA-certified lab for confirmation testing. If it comes back negative, the result is canceled.

How long does a DOT violation stay on my record?

DOT drug and alcohol violations remain in the FMCSA Clearinghouse until you complete the full return-to-duty process, and for 5 years after the violation date (whichever is later).

Can I drive for a different company after a positive DOT test?

No. All DOT violations are in the FMCSA Clearinghouse. Every prospective employer must conduct a pre-employment query. They will see your violation and cannot hire you for safety-sensitive functions until you complete the RTD process.
