DOT Return to Duty Process: Everything You Need to Know

After a DOT drug or alcohol violation, federal law requires completing the full Return to Duty (RTD) process before you can drive again. Here is every step — what it is, how long it takes, and what happens if you skip one.

⚠️

You cannot drive until this process is complete. Operating a CMV after a confirmed violation — before passing the RTD drug test — is a federal offense. Do not return to work until your SAP clears you and you have a negative RTD test result in hand.

What Triggers the Return to Duty Process

Any of the following DOT violations requires completing the full RTD process before you can drive a commercial motor vehicle again:

Positive Drug Test
DOT urine test confirmed positive by MRO for any of the 5-panel substances
Refusal to Test
No-show, test refusal, adulterated or substituted specimen
Alcohol ≥ 0.04
Breath alcohol concentration at or above 0.04 on an evidential breath test
Actual Knowledge
Employer-observed drug or alcohol use, or admission of use before a test

The 5 Required Steps

All five steps must be completed in order. Skipping any step means you cannot legally return to driving.

1
Step 1 — Immediate

Removed from Safety-Sensitive Duties

The moment a violation is confirmed (positive MRO-verified result, refusal, or ≥0.04 alcohol), your employer must immediately remove you from all safety-sensitive functions. You cannot drive, load/unload hazmat, or perform any regulated duty. This is not optional for the employer.

2
Step 2 — First Action

SAP Initial Evaluation

You must see a DOT-qualified Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) for a face-to-face clinical evaluation. The SAP assesses the violation, your history, and your situation — then prescribes a specific education or treatment plan. The SAP's recommendation is mandatory; you cannot negotiate it down.

What SAPs look for
Prior violations, severity of substance use, personal circumstances. Education-only is possible for first-time minor violations. Treatment programs are more common for positive tests.
3
Step 3 — Completion Required

Education or Treatment

You must complete whatever the SAP prescribed — a substance abuse education program, outpatient treatment, inpatient treatment, or some combination. The timeline here is entirely driven by the SAP's plan.

Education Only
Typically 1–4 weeks
Treatment Program
4 weeks to several months
4
Step 4 — Critical Gate

SAP Follow-Up Evaluation & RTD Drug Test

After completing the prescribed program, you return to the SAP for a follow-up evaluation. If the SAP determines you have complied with all recommendations, they authorize a return-to-duty drug and/or alcohol test.

The RTD drug test must be negative
This test is directly observed. If you fail, the process restarts from SAP evaluation. There is no limit on how many times this can happen.

The RTD test is a standard DOT 5-panel urine test, but it is directly observed collection. It must be performed at a DOT-certified collection site with a certified collector.

5
Step 5 — Ongoing

Follow-Up Testing Plan

The SAP creates a follow-up testing plan — the minimum is 6 unannounced tests in the first 12 months back at work. The plan can extend up to 60 months. Testing is unannounced; you will not know when the next test is coming.

Clearinghouse entry
The SAP reports RTD completion to the FMCSA Clearinghouse. The original violation stays in the Clearinghouse for 5 years. Future employers will see both the violation and that you completed the RTD process.

Return to Duty FAQ

Can I choose my own SAP? +

Yes. You have the right to choose any DOT-qualified SAP. Your employer can provide a list, but cannot require you to use a specific one. The SAP must be listed on a legitimate DOT SAP roster and hold the required credentials (licensed counselor, social worker, psychologist, or physician with substance abuse specialty).

What if I change employers during the RTD process? +

The violation follows you via the FMCSA Clearinghouse. Any new employer running a pre-employment query will see the violation. You cannot bypass the RTD process by switching employers. The new employer will see the incomplete RTD status and cannot put you in a safety-sensitive role until RTD is completed.

Who pays for the RTD process? +

Federal regulations do not specify who pays. Most employers require the driver to pay for SAP evaluation and treatment. The RTD drug test is typically the driver's responsibility. Some employer assistance programs (EAPs) may cover some costs. Check your employment agreement.

Is the RTD drug test observed? +

Yes. The return-to-duty drug test is always a directly observed collection. A same-gender collector will directly observe the urine specimen entering the collection container. This is required by 49 CFR Part 40 and cannot be waived.

What happens if I fail the RTD drug test? +

A failed RTD test is a new DOT violation and is reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse as a separate violation. The entire process starts over — you must return to the SAP for another evaluation, complete any new recommendations, and pass another RTD test before returning to work.

Does a DOT violation on my record mean I can never drive again? +

No. Completing the RTD process fully — SAP evaluation, treatment, negative RTD test, employer clearance, follow-up plan — allows you to return to commercial driving. The violation stays in the Clearinghouse for 5 years but the completed RTD is also recorded, showing future employers that you addressed the violation properly.
