What Is the FMCSA Clearinghouse?

The FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse (formally the Commercial Driver's License Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse) is a secure federal online database administered by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. It launched January 6, 2020.

The database contains records of all DOT drug and alcohol program violations committed by CDL holders. Employers must query it before hiring any CDL driver and annually for all current drivers. Drivers can see their own records at any time.

Penalties for non-compliance: up to $16,000 per violation

Employers who fail to query the Clearinghouse before hiring a CDL driver, or who fail to run annual queries on current drivers, are subject to federal civil penalties.

Who Must Register

CDL Drivers

All CDL holders must register to provide consent for employer queries and to view their own violation records.

Employers

All FMCSA-regulated employers must register to run pre-employment and annual queries on CDL drivers.

C/TPAs

Consortia and third-party administrators register to run queries and submit violations on behalf of employers they serve.

MROs & SAPs

Medical Review Officers and Substance Abuse Professionals register to report violations and RTD completion.

What Gets Reported to the Clearinghouse

✗
Positive Drug Test
Any DOT 5-panel urine test confirmed positive by MRO
✗
Alcohol Test ≥ 0.04
Breath alcohol result at or above 0.04 on an evidential breath test
✗
Refusal to Test
No-show, test refusal, adulterated specimen, or substituted specimen
✗
Actual Knowledge Violations
Employer knowledge of driver under influence, or positive test result admission
✓
RTD Completion (Positive Entry)
SAP reports when driver completes return-to-duty process and is cleared to drive

Query Types: Full vs. Limited

Full Query
Required Before Hire

Returns the driver's complete Clearinghouse record. Requires the driver's electronic consent via the Clearinghouse portal before you can view results.

When: Before putting any CDL driver behind the wheel
Limited Query
Required Annually for Current Drivers

Shows only whether a violation exists (yes/no), not the details. If a violation is flagged, a full query is required. Driver provides blanket consent at hire.

When: Once per year for every current CDL driver

How Long Do Violations Stay in the Clearinghouse?

Violations remain in the Clearinghouse for 5 years from the date the violation was entered, or until the driver completes the return-to-duty (RTD) process — whichever is longer. After RTD completion AND 5 years have elapsed, FMCSA removes the violation from the record.

How to resolve a Clearinghouse violation

A driver with a Clearinghouse violation must complete the full DOT Return-to-Duty (RTD) process, which includes evaluation by a Substance Abuse Professional (SAP), required treatment/education, a return-to-duty drug test, and follow-up testing.

FMCSA Clearinghouse FAQ

Is the FMCSA Clearinghouse the same as a background check?

No. The Clearinghouse is specifically for DOT drug and alcohol violations by CDL drivers. It does not contain criminal records, traffic violations, or non-DOT drug test results. It's a separate and additional requirement from standard employment background checks.

Can a driver with a Clearinghouse violation drive?

No. A driver with an unresolved violation in the Clearinghouse is prohibited from performing safety-sensitive functions (driving a CMV). They must complete the full return-to-duty process before they can drive commercially again.

Do owner-operators need to worry about the Clearinghouse?

Yes. Owner-operators must register as both driver AND employer in the Clearinghouse. They must also be enrolled in a DOT consortium (C/TPA) that runs annual queries on their behalf. Our consortium handles Clearinghouse compliance as part of enrollment.

What happens if an employer hires a driver without querying the Clearinghouse?

Federal civil penalties apply — up to $16,000 per violation. The employer may also be liable if the driver is involved in an accident while prohibited from driving. Pre-employment queries are not optional.
