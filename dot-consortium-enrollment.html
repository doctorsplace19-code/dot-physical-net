Who Is Required to Be in a Consortium?

Any FMCSA-regulated employer who operates fewer than the statistically valid minimum of drivers to run their own standalone random program must join a consortium. In practice, this means:

Owner-Operators

A single CDL driver who operates under their own USDOT authority. Cannot self-administer a random program — consortium enrollment is mandatory.

Small Carriers (1–4 drivers)

Small fleets often join consortiums because managing an in-house random program for just a few drivers is impractical and may not meet statistical requirements.

Leased Drivers

A CDL driver leased to a carrier is subject to the carrier's drug testing program. If there is no carrier program, the driver must be in a consortium under their own authority.

New Motor Carriers

All new FMCSA-regulated carriers must establish a drug and alcohol testing program before any driver performs a safety-sensitive function. Consortium enrollment satisfies this requirement.

Penalty for operating without consortium enrollment

Operating a CMV in commerce without being enrolled in a random drug testing program violates 49 CFR Part 382. DOT civil penalties: up to $16,000 per violation per day. FMCSA may also issue an out-of-service order, preventing the carrier from operating until compliant.

How DOT Random Testing Works Inside a Consortium

The FMCSA sets minimum annual random selection rates across the entire pool. Here is what happens from enrollment to test completion:

1
You are enrolled in the consortium pool
Your name and CDL go into the random selection database. You are eligible for selection from the moment enrollment is confirmed.
2
The consortium runs computer-generated random selections quarterly
A scientifically random process selects names from the pool at FMCSA-required rates: 50% of the pool per year for drugs, 10% for alcohol. Each driver's odds reset each selection period.
3
You receive a selection notice — test immediately
If selected, you must proceed to a collection site immediately (before driving, same day) unless special circumstances apply. You cannot refuse or delay without it being treated as a refusal.
4
Lab tests the specimen, MRO reviews the result
A SAMHSA-certified lab tests the urine specimen. A certified MRO reviews all non-negative results and contacts you for a donor interview before any result is verified. See MRO timeline →
5
Result reported — clearinghouse updated if non-negative
Negative results are reported to your employer (or to you as the owner-operator). Non-negatives are reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse and trigger immediate removal from safety-sensitive duties.

FMCSA Random Testing Rates

Test Type Current Annual Rate Substances Tested
Random Drug Test 50% of pool/year Marijuana, cocaine, opiates/opioids, amphetamines, PCP
Random Alcohol Test 10% of pool/year Blood alcohol content (BAC) — 0.02 warning, 0.04 violation
Pre-employment Drug Test Required once Same 5-panel — required before first safety-sensitive duty

Rates may be adjusted by FMCSA based on industry-wide positive test rates. Consortiums apply these rates across the entire pool, so individual selection probability equals the rate.

Consortium Enrollment FAQ

How quickly am I enrolled and in the random pool? +

Online enrollment is immediate — you are added to the pool upon payment confirmation. You will receive a membership confirmation you can show during inspections. Random selection happens on a quarterly cycle, so your first chance of being selected begins with the next selection run.

What happens if I am selected for a random test while I am not driving? +

A random test is only required when you are performing or about to perform a safety-sensitive function (pre-duty, during a duty period). If you receive a random selection notice and you are not scheduled to drive, you are not required to test immediately — but you must test the next time you are performing safety-sensitive duties, before you drive.

Can I use any collection site with the consortium? +

Yes — DOT-regulated collections can be performed at any SAMHSA-certified collection site using the federal chain-of-custody form. Your consortium provides the paperwork or order code, and you can go to any certified clinic, urgent care, or occupational health center nationwide. DotPhysical.net has collection sites in all 50 states.

Do I need a new consortium if I change my USDOT number? +

If you are issued a new USDOT number (e.g., you changed business entity), you need to update your consortium enrollment with the new number. Contact your consortium immediately. Driving under a new USDOT without updated consortium enrollment creates a compliance gap.
