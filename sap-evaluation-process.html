DOT SAP Evaluation Process: What to Expect

After a DOT drug or alcohol violation, a Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) evaluation is required by federal law before you can return to any safety-sensitive duties. Here is exactly what the SAP evaluation involves, how long the process takes, and what happens at each step.

You cannot drive until this process is complete. No exceptions — no driving, no safety-sensitive functions of any kind.

What Happens at the SAP Evaluation

The SAP evaluation is a clinical interview — not a treatment session. The SAP's job is to assess your situation and prescribe what level of intervention is appropriate. The evaluation itself typically takes 1–3 hours and covers:

Circumstances of the Violation

What happened, which test type, what was found. The SAP reviews the MRO's report and violation documentation.

Substance Use History

How long, how often, what substances. The SAP is specifically trained to assess the clinical picture — be honest.

Prior Treatment History

Any prior substance use evaluation, counseling, treatment, or AA/NA participation is reviewed and factored into the recommendation.

General Mental Health History

Co-occurring mental health concerns (depression, anxiety, PTSD) are part of the clinical picture and may affect the treatment recommendation.

What the SAP prescribes

After the evaluation, the SAP prescribes one of several levels of care: a substance use education program only, outpatient counseling, intensive outpatient (IOP), or residential treatment. For a first-time, isolated violation (especially if the driver has no pattern of use), education-only is common. For more significant substance involvement, treatment is required. The SAP's clinical judgment drives the prescription — it is not negotiable.

SAP Evaluation FAQ

Is the SAP evaluation information confidential from my employer? +

Yes. Your substance use history, clinical notes, and the content of your evaluation are confidential medical information. The SAP communicates to your employer only: (1) whether you have been evaluated, (2) whether you have complied with the treatment recommendation, and (3) whether you are eligible for a return-to-duty test. The employer does not see clinical details, diagnosis, or what was discussed.

Can I get a second opinion on the SAP's treatment recommendation? +

No. DOT regulations require that you follow the SAP's recommended treatment program before you can take a return-to-duty test. You cannot see a different SAP and seek a less intensive recommendation — the first SAP's prescription stands. The only recourse is to appeal through your employer's process if you believe the SAP was not qualified or did not follow DOT protocols, which is a high bar.

Does the SAP evaluation go on my permanent record? +

The underlying violation is reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse and stays there for 5 years. The violation record is visible to any employer who queries you in the Clearinghouse. The SAP evaluation and follow-up status are also reported to and tracked in the Clearinghouse. Prospective employers will see that there was a violation, the RTD process was completed, and the current status.

What is follow-up testing and how long does it last? +

After passing your RTD drug test, the SAP prescribes a follow-up testing plan: minimum 6 unannounced tests in the first 12 months. The SAP can extend this up to 60 months based on clinical judgment. Follow-up tests are separate from random pool testing — you will receive both. Your consortium or C/TPA administers the follow-up schedule and notifies you when tests are required.
