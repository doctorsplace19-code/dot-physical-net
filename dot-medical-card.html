What Is a DOT Medical Card?

A DOT medical card — formally called the Medical Examiner's Certificate (MEC) — is a wallet-sized certificate issued by an FMCSA-registered Federal Medical Examiner after you pass a DOT physical exam. Federal law (49 CFR Part 391.41) requires all commercial motor vehicle (CMV) drivers to hold a valid DOT medical card.

The card shows:

  • ✓Your name and date of birth
  • ✓The examiner's National Registry number
  • ✓Issue date and expiration date
  • ✓Any applicable restrictions (e.g., must wear corrective lenses, requires hearing aid)
Important: CDL Medical Certification Required

Commercial drivers must submit their Medical Examiner's Certificate to their state DMV. Your CDL will reflect a "certified" medical status. Failure to submit — or operating with an expired card — is a federal violation that can result in your CDL being downgraded to a non-CDL license.

How Long Is a DOT Medical Card Valid?

The standard validity period is 24 months. However, the examiner can issue a shorter certificate based on your health status:

Normal BP, no conditions24 monthsStandard certification

Where to Get a DOT Medical Card

Your DOT physical — and therefore your medical card — must be performed by a Federal Medical Examiner (FME) listed on the FMCSA National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners. Not every doctor qualifies. A regular family physician who is not on the FMCSA registry cannot certify you.

DOT Medical Card FAQ

Can I get a DOT medical card with high blood pressure?

Yes, in most cases. Stage 1 (140–159/90–99) and Stage 2 (160–179/100–109) hypertension result in a 12-month certificate. Stage 3 (180/110+) is disqualifying until treated to below 180/110. Controlled blood pressure with medication is certifiable.

What happens if my DOT medical card expires while driving?

Operating a CMV with an expired medical card is a federal violation. Your CDL can be downgraded and you can be placed out of service. Renew before expiration — you can renew up to 90 days early without losing any validity time.

Can I get a DOT medical card with diabetes?

Non-insulin-treated diabetes is generally certifiable. Insulin-treated diabetes mellitus (ITDM) requires an FMCSA exemption based on documented stable control and specialist letters.

Do I need to submit my DOT medical card to the DMV?

Yes. Federal law requires commercial drivers to self-certify with their state licensing agency and submit their medical certificate. Failure to do so can result in your CDL being downgraded to a non-CDL license.

How much does a DOT medical card cost?

The DOT physical exam that results in your medical card costs $110 at DotPhysical.net. If you pass, your Medical Examiner's Certificate is issued the same day at no additional charge.

Is my DOT medical card the same as a CDL physical card?

Yes — "CDL medical card," "DOT medical card," "DOT physical card," and "Medical Examiner's Certificate" all refer to the same document. It is issued after passing your DOT physical exam.
