Adderall and DOT Drug Tests: What CDL Drivers Need to Know

Adderall is an amphetamine — it will flag on a DOT 5-panel drug test. But having a valid prescription does not automatically mean a positive result. Here is exactly what happens and what you need to do.

MRO CALLS YOU

DOT PHYSICAL

What Happens When Adderall Flags on a DOT Test

Adderall contains amphetamine and dextroamphetamine — both are included in the DOT 5-panel urine drug test. If you take Adderall, it will produce a non-negative screen result for amphetamines, which then goes to confirmatory GC/MS testing. The lab confirms the presence of amphetamine and sends the result to your MRO.

1
Lab flags non-negative, runs GC/MS confirmation
The immunoassay screen detects amphetamines. GC/MS confirms the specific substance. Adderall will confirm positive for amphetamine. This is sent to the MRO — not to your employer.
2
MRO calls you directly — answer the phone
The Medical Review Officer calls you privately for a donor interview. They will not contact your employer first. Answer an unknown number — it may be the MRO. They must attempt contact multiple times; unanswered calls eventually lead them to involve your employer's DER.
3
Disclose your prescription during the interview
Tell the MRO you have a valid prescription for Adderall. Provide the prescribing doctor's name, approximate date of prescription, and pharmacy if asked. You may be asked to provide documentation — a prescription bottle label or a letter from your doctor.
4
MRO makes the final determination
If the prescription is valid and current, the MRO may report the result as negative (valid medical explanation). If there is no valid prescription or the MRO has concerns, the result is reported as positive. The employer sees only the final verified result — not the prescription details.

Adderall at the DOT Physical — a Separate Issue

The drug test and the DOT physical are two separate evaluations. Even if the MRO reports your drug test as negative, the medical examiner conducting your DOT physical independently evaluates whether you are medically fit to drive.

What the medical examiner evaluates for Adderall/ADHD
› Whether the ADHD diagnosis and medication are documented and managed by a treating physician
› Whether Adderall causes cardiovascular side effects (elevated BP, elevated heart rate) at the exam
› Whether there are signs of impaired judgment, sleep disruption, or anxiety from the medication
› Whether the ADHD itself (not just the medication) affects driving safety
Bring a letter from your prescribing physician

A letter from the doctor who prescribes your Adderall — confirming the diagnosis, that the medication is appropriate, that you have no impairing side effects, and that your condition is stable — significantly helps the examiner make an informed determination. This is not required but highly recommended.

Adderall & DOT Drug Test FAQ

Will the MRO tell my employer I take Adderall? +

No. The MRO is legally prohibited from sharing your prescription or medical information with your employer. The employer receives only the final result — negative or positive. If the MRO verifies your prescription as valid and reports a negative, your employer is told "negative." Your Adderall prescription is never disclosed.

What if I took Adderall that was not prescribed to me? +

If you do not have a valid prescription in your name, the MRO cannot verify a legitimate medical explanation and will report the result as positive. A positive DOT drug test result triggers immediate removal from safety-sensitive duties and the return-to-duty process. Only a prescription in your name, current and valid at the time of the test, provides the basis for the MRO to report a negative.

What about Vyvanse, Ritalin, or other ADHD medications? +

Vyvanse (lisdexamfetamine) converts to amphetamine in the body and will also flag on the amphetamine panel — same process applies. Ritalin and Concerta (methylphenidate) are in a different drug class and do NOT appear on the standard DOT 5-panel amphetamine screen. However, methylphenidate has its own considerations for the DOT physical medical evaluation.

Can I stop taking Adderall before a drug test? +

DOT random drug tests are unannounced — you cannot predict when one will occur, so planning around your medication is not practical. More importantly: if you have a valid prescription and take your medication as directed, the MRO process is designed exactly for this situation. Stopping a prescribed medication without medical supervision can also be unsafe. Take your medication as prescribed and let the MRO process work.

I failed a DOT drug test for amphetamines — what now? +

If a positive was verified and reported, the return-to-duty process applies. You must complete SAP evaluation, any prescribed treatment or education, and pass a return-to-duty drug test before returning to safety-sensitive duties.
